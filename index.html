      Method

      For every point we take 20-year monthly averages (2001–2020) from NASA POWER: temperature, rain, humidity, air pressure, UV, cloud cover, sunlight and wind.

      No place on Earth reaches Mars or Moon values, so each factor scores how far a place goes in the target's direction compared with the rest of Earth's land (a percentile from 0 to 1). A place drier than 98% of land scores 0.98 for dryness.

      The twin score is the weighted average of the factors. Weights come from the mission profile and can be changed with the sliders. Every place gets a score for every month, which gives its best test window.

      We search systematically: 1,287 land points on a 4° global grid, then a 1° grid around the strongest regions. Eight well-known analog sites are scored the same way as benchmarks.

      Earth's limits

      No place on Earth reproduces low gravity, the lunar vacuum, the thin Martian atmosphere or their radiation. Every site lists what it is not valid for.

      Target reference values

      • Mars: surface pressure about 6.36 mbar (0.64 kPa, less than 1% of Earth's), average temperature about −59 °C, daily range −89 to −31 °C at the Viking 1 site. NASA Mars Fact Sheet
      • Moon, south pole: no atmosphere; permanently shadowed crater floors reach about −250 °C (25 K), the coldest temperature measured in the solar system. NASA LRO: Temperature Variation on the Moon

      These values are far outside anything on Earth, which is why the app scores direction and rank instead of matching numbers.

      Data and sources

      • NASA POWER Climatology API, 20-year monthly averages 2001–2020 (MERRA-2, CERES)
      • NASA Worldview satellite imagery for the surface check of each candidate site
      • NASA GIBS Blue Marble map tiles
      • LRO Diviner polar products (NASA PDS) for the Moon reference
      • NASA Mars Fact Sheet for the Mars reference
      • Map library: Leaflet

      Limitations

      • Climate data is averaged over grid cells about 0.5–1° wide, so local conditions (a single crater floor) can differ.
      • Geology and terrain are not scored automatically; they come from the Worldview surface check.
      • Scores are relative to Earth's land, not absolute similarity to Mars or the Moon.
